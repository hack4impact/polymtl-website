The organization

The Réseau Canopée is a nonprofit in Laval that protects and restores natural areas. It plants trees, runs conservation projects and does environmental education.

The organization has a small staff and volunteer patrollers who monitor about eight wooded areas.

The problem

Before the project, Canopée managed its patrols and reports with several separate tools. Excel files, a Google Form, social media, Google Maps and email.

Patrol routes were not recorded. Two volunteers could walk the same area on the same day while other areas went unvisited, and the team had no overall view of the most visited zones.

Reports came in by message or email, with photos but no common format. There was no way to know if an issue had been fixed. Wildlife and plant observations were spread across several Excel files, and volunteer accounts were managed by hand.

Approach

The project started with meetings with the Canopée team to understand the patrollers' work. These needs were gathered in a product requirements document (PRD), where each feature had an hour estimate and a priority.

Design followed in three steps. A moodboard first set the visual direction, based on Canopée's identity. Low-fidelity (lo-fi) wireframes then laid out the screens and validated the user flows with the organization. High-fidelity (hi-fi) designs finally defined the colors, typography and components before development.

Development was done in sprints. Every pull request went through a code review, and automated tests were written with Vitest. The database schema came first, then the map, patrols and reports were added one by one.

iOS and Android versions were then built to solve the limits of the web version, such as GPS tracking stopping when the screen is locked.

Features

The app has three main parts. Patrols, reports and administration.

9:41

Map

The map shows the wooded areas, open reports and a heat map of the zones volunteers visit most. Reports can be filtered by category, and administrators can also filter by date range.

Wildlife and plant observations are only visible to staff, so the location of threatened species is not made public.

9:41

Reports

There are three types of reports. Maintenance, for example a dangerous tree or a blocked trail. Citizen, for example an unleashed dog or a campfire. Wildlife and plants, to record an observed species. The location is added automatically and every report gets a number.

For wildlife and plants, the provincial list of species at risk is built in. A species can be found by its common or scientific name. Citizens can also submit a report without an account.

9:41

Patrols

The patroller taps a button to start a patrol. The app records their location while they walk. At the end, they see the duration, distance and route. Other volunteers' ongoing patrols are also visible on the map.

On iOS and Android, recording keeps going even when the screen is locked. Without network, points are kept on the phone and sent later.

9:41

Administration

When a volunteer creates an account, an administrator has to approve it. Staff can mark a report as resolved. It no longer shows on the map but stays in the database.

Challenges

Network in the forest

The network is often weak in wooded areas. Offline mode had to be handled for patrols and reports.

GPS accuracy

Some positions are off by several dozen meters. Inaccurate points are filtered out to keep routes clean.

Background tracking

iOS and Android often stop apps running in the background. Native code in Swift and Java was needed so patrol recording keeps going with the screen locked.
